CSCI 4750/5750 · Spring 2027 · Lecture Schedule

42 class meetings (Mon/Wed/Fri 10:00–10:50), Mon Jan 11 – Mon May 3, plus the registrar’s final-exam slot (Wed May 5, 12:00–1:50 PM). Project presentations run on three days: Fri Apr 30 and Mon May 3 (in class) and Wed May 5 (exam slot). Key dates per the official SLU 2026–27 academic calendar and the registrar’s final-exam table: no classes Mon Jan 18 (MLK Day), Wed Feb 10 (Wellness Day), Mar 8–12 (Spring Break), and Fri Mar 26 + Mon Mar 29 (Easter Break); SLU mid-term exam week Mar 1–5; classes end Mon May 3; Study Day Tue May 4; final exams May 5–11.

Readings: [ISLP n] = James, Witten, Hastie, Tibshirani & Taylor, An Introduction to Statistical Learning with Applications in Python (2023), chapter n; [PML n] = Murphy, Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction (2022); [MML n] = Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, Mathematics for Machine Learning (2020); [UDL n] = Prince, Understanding Deep Learning (2023); [S&B n] = Sutton & Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd ed. (2018). All five are free online (links in the syllabus). Papers are the primary reading where listed.

Every lecture ends its main content with a “Now” segment: where the day’s classical idea lives in 2026–27 industry or research. Those connections are listed in italics below.

Subject to change. I’ll tune pacing and topics as I learn what works for this class, and the “Now” readings especially are tentative. Canvas always has the most current version of everything; watch announcements there for updates.

Unit 1 — Foundations: Learning from Data

# Date Topic & key ideas Reading Out / Due
1 Mon Jan 11 Welcome: what is machine learning? Programs that learn from examples; supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning; the recipe every method follows (data, model, loss, optimizer, evaluation); course map; logistics; background survey via in-class questions. Now: one system everyone has used, taken apart into this course’s lectures. ISLP 1; PML 1
2 Wed Jan 13 The learning problem & nearest neighbors. Features, labels, hypotheses; loss, risk and empirical risk; train/test splits; k-NN classification and regression worked by hand; distance choices; the curse of dimensionality. Now: vector search behind recommendation and retrieval is nearest neighbors at billion scale. ISLP 2; PML 16.1
3 Fri Jan 15 Linear algebra for ML. Data as a matrix; vectors, dot products, norms, angles; matrix–vector products as linear maps; independence, rank, inverses; projections; a first look at eigenvectors. Now: embeddings and cosine similarity. MML 2.1–2.6, 3.1–3.4, 3.8 HW1 out
4 Wed Jan 20 Calculus & optimization for ML. Gradients and the chain rule in vector form; Jacobians and Hessians in brief; convexity; gradient descent and the step size; local vs. global minima. Now: a frontier model’s training run is this loop, repeated about a million times. MML 5.1–5.3, 7.1, 7.3
5 Fri Jan 22 Linear regression I: least squares. Model and squared loss; the normal equations on a three-point example; geometry: projection onto the column space; probability: Gaussian noise makes least squares the maximum-likelihood fit; R². Now: linear probes that read concepts out of large models. ISLP 3.1–3.2; MML 9.1–9.2
6 Mon Jan 25 Linear regression II: gradient descent, SGD & features. Closed form vs. iterative cost; SGD and mini-batches; learning-rate pathologies; feature scaling; polynomial and basis features; encoding categories. Now: batch sizes of millions of tokens. ISLP 3.3, 7.1; PML 8.2, 8.4 Project team sign-up opens (Canvas)
7 Wed Jan 27 Generalization: overfitting, bias–variance & cross-validation. Training vs. test error; the polynomial-degree story; the bias–variance decomposition; validation sets and k-fold CV; learning curves. Now: double descent, and why huge networks do not overfit the way the U-curve predicts. ISLP 2.2, 5.1; UDL 8
8 Fri Jan 29 Regularization: ridge, lasso & priors. Ridge in closed form; lasso and sparsity, drawn; regularization paths; choosing λ by cross-validation; MAP view: Gaussian prior ↔︎ ridge, Laplace prior ↔︎ lasso. Now: weight decay in AdamW, in every LLM training recipe. ISLP 6.2; PML 11.3–11.4

Recurring threads (called out across lectures)

  • The recipe card: every method is data + model + loss + optimizer + evaluation. L1 introduces the card; L5 (regression), L9 (logistic), L13 (SVM), L15 (trees), L26 (neural nets), L31 (self-supervised), L33 (diffusion) and L34–35 (RL) each fill in a new row.
  • Generalization: L2 (the k in k-NN) → L7 (bias–variance) → L8 (regularization) → L16–17 (ensembles) → L18 (theory) → L28 (deep nets and double descent).
  • Every loss is a likelihood: L5 (Gaussian noise → squared error) → L8 (priors → penalties) → L9 (Bernoulli → cross-entropy) → L11–12 (generative models, Bayes) → L21 (latent variables, EM) → L25 (graphical models) → L32–33 (deep generative models).
  • Geometry: L3 → L5 (projection) → L13 (margins) → L14 (kernels) → L22 (PCA) → L23 (embeddings) → L31 (contrastive learning).
  • The Fashion-MNIST scoreboard: one dataset, one test set, re-measured all semester: k-NN (L2, HW1) → softmax regression (L9, HW2) → random forest (L16) → PCA + k-NN (L22, HW3) → MLP (L26, HW4) → CNN (L29, HW4) → a linear probe on a pretrained network (L31, HW4).
  • Evaluation honesty: L7 (cross-validation) → L10 (metrics, base rates) → L19 (leakage) → L36 (fairness metrics) → L37 (distribution shift).
  • Responsible ML: L1 → L10 (base rates in screening) → L19 (leakage) → L24 (feedback loops) → L36 (fairness) → L37 (deployment).
